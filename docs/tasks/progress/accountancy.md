## Build-wide content constraints

- This is an introductory orientation to the accountancy profession and its foundations, not accounting, tax, or financial advice and not board exam review.
- The first lesson in every course must state: “Educational orientation, not accounting, tax, or financial advice.”
- Keep regulatory and professional references at name level only. In Path to Practice, mention that accountants in the Philippines take the Certified Public Accountant licensure examination administered by the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), and that the profession is overseen by the Board of Accountancy. Direct learners to official PRC, Board of Accountancy, and CHED sources for current requirements.
- Mention that financial reporting follows recognized standards in The Accountancy Profession and Ethics, Understanding Financial Statements, and Introduction to Auditing and Assurance. Do not name standards or state what they require; direct learners to official sources for current reporting rules.
- In Introduction to Taxation only, say that tax rules are set by law and administered by the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR). Explain the topic as something learners will study in depth later and direct learners to official sources for current rules.
- Make ethics, integrity, and confidentiality recurring themes, with each course applying them to its own subject rather than repeating the same lesson.
- Journal entries and ledgers in Recording Transactions, and statements in Understanding Financial Statements, use small invented examples with made-up businesses and round numbers, clearly labelled as examples.
- Keep the tracks distinct: Accounting Foundations covers concepts, recording, and reporting; Accounting in Practice covers decisions, assurance, tax, business finance, controls, and career directions. Avoid duplicate or near-duplicate lessons, especially across Recording Transactions and Bookkeeping for Small Businesses, The Accountancy Profession and Ethics and Internal Controls and Fraud Awareness, and Understanding Financial Statements and Business and Finance Basics.
- Use only `quickCheck`, `order`, and `matchPairs` activities. Do not use code playgrounds.
- Supplied links are empty. Do not invent video or reference URLs.
- Exclude tax rates, thresholds, deadlines, forms, filing steps, and advice applicable to a reader's own taxes or business; specific standard numbers, rule citations, or claims about what a standard requires; statistics, salaries, hiring or overseas-demand claims, firm rankings, exam dates, fees, passing rates, exam coverage, or agency order numbers; legal-liability or legal-scope-of-practice claims; and investment advice or product recommendations.
- This is a review-required career. Obtain review from an accountancy student, CPA, or accountancy instructor before build; keep the career `coming-soon` until a human changes its status after review of the built content. Do not claim expert review has happened.

### Course 1: The Accountancy Profession and Ethics

**Course ID:** `acct-profession-ethics` · **Short code:** `APE` · **Lessons:** 10  
**Subtitle:** What accountants do and the trust they carry.  
**Outcome:** Describe the roles of accountants and why integrity, objectivity, and confidentiality matter.

1. **An Orientation to Accountancy** — State “Educational orientation, not accounting, tax, or financial advice” and distinguish general learning from individualized professional advice.
2. **What Accounting Helps People Understand** — Explain how organized financial information can help different users understand an organization's activities.
3. **The Accountant's Work in Context** — Compare broad tasks such as organizing information, communicating summaries, and supporting responsible decisions in different work contexts.
4. **Integrity in Everyday Work** — Choose a transparent response when a fictional record appears incomplete or inconsistent.
5. **Objectivity and Fair Judgment** — Explain how considering evidence rather than personal preference supports a balanced conclusion.
6. **Confidentiality and Respectful Information Use** — Identify why financial details should be shared only with appropriate people for a legitimate purpose.
7. **Financial Reporting and Recognized Standards** — Explain at a high level that financial reporting follows recognized standards, is studied in greater depth later, and requires consulting official sources for current reporting rules.
8. **Clear Communication and Documentation** — Describe how precise, understandable explanations help users distinguish recorded facts from assumptions.
9. **Working with Others and Knowing Limits** — Explain why accountants may collaborate with other roles and seek qualified guidance when a question exceeds their knowledge.
10. **Trust as a Professional Habit** — Connect integrity, objectivity, confidentiality, and careful communication in a fictional workplace scenario.
